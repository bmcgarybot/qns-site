01 / THE THING YOU DID

You see their face again.

You said it. You saw their face drop. Maybe you apologized. Maybe they told you it was fine. But years later, you still remember the look on their face and wonder why you had to be that person in that moment.

02 / THE THING THEY SAID

You still have the message.

You could delete it. You could stop opening it. But you remember what it says anyway. Sometimes you think about what you should have said back. Sometimes you get angry all over again, sitting in a room where nothing is actually happening.

03 / THE CONVERSATION YOU CAN'T HAVE

There isn't going to be an answer.

Maybe they died. Maybe you stopped speaking. Maybe getting back in touch would be the worst thing you could do. You still want an apology, an explanation, or one chance to say what you never got to say.

What is this costing you now?

It happened then.
It still gets a piece of today.

Not every minute. Not every day. But enough that you know what this feels like.

Your sleep

You're tired.
Then you remember.

You finally get into bed. You were exhausted five minutes ago. Now you're wide awake, going over what you should have said while the clock keeps moving.

The people in front of you

They're here.
Your head is back there.

Your partner is talking. Your kid wants to show you something. You hear them, but you're still arguing with somebody who isn't even in the room.

How you see yourself

You've changed.
You still hold it against yourself.

You wouldn't make that choice today. You know that. Then the memory comes back and you start talking to yourself like you're still the person who did it.

How much longer do you want to keep having this conversation in your head?

The part we can actually work on.

You can't unsend it.
You can't un-say it.
You can't make them answer.

You may still have something to make right. You may need to keep a boundary. Forgiveness doesn't make either of those disappear.

But replaying it tonight won't change what happened. Hating yourself for it won't give you a different past. Staying angry won't make them finally understand.

So what do you do with what is still there?

No. You can participate quietly without telling your story. Keep other people's names, identifying information and private details out of group posts and discussion. Passing, pausing and stopping are always allowed.
